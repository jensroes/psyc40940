# Week 4 Notes For Next Year

- The 2026 session felt repetitive in places. Review overlapping explanations across the slides and exercises, and reduce repetition so each example adds a distinct point.
- Simplify exercise code in Weeks 1-4 by removing customisation tasks that belong in Week 5, such as changing themes and adding polished axis labels, titles, or legends. Keep student edits focused on the week's core concepts. Where readable labels are needed to interpret an example, provide them rather than making customisation an additional task.
- Keep the trade-off between statistical summaries and raw data as a central teaching idea. Summaries make patterns easier to see but conceal individual observations and variation; raw data reveal those details but can make overall patterns difficult to identify. Explore when combining them helps.
- Redesign Exercise 1 around meaningful choices between plausible alternatives. Avoid comparisons where one option clearly cannot answer the question.
- Useful comparisons include a histogram versus a density plot for a distribution, and a scatterplot versus two-dimensional density contours for a relationship. Ask what becomes easier to see and what is concealed in each version.
- Use less written reflection and more blanks in working code. Students should complete mappings, geom names, or relevant arguments and see the effect on the figure.
- Let students switch between alternatives by commenting and uncommenting geom lines. Their choice should change the plot, rather than just assign an unused response variable.
- Keep the question and data consistent across each comparison, with common scales where appropriate. Students can briefly explain or discuss their preferred option without writing a separate response for every small step.
