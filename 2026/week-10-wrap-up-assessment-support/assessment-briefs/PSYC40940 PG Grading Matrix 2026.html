Module Learning Outcomes

MLOFormal learning outcomeWhat this means for the portfolio
MLO1Demonstrate a critical understanding of the general principles of effective data visualisation.Use visualisation principles to make justified design choices. Explain why each plot type, encoding, scale, label, colour choice, and layout helps the reader understand the behavioural data without misleading them.
MLO2Use state-of-the-art and industry-standard tools to produce high-quality, advanced visualisations of a range of large or complex data sets.Use appropriate R tools, such as RMarkdown, ggplot2, specialist visualisation packages, and Shiny, to work with a suitably complex behavioural dataset and produce technically competent visual outputs.
MLO3Use state-of-the-art and industry-standard tools to produce clear, publication-quality figures that adhere to the principles of reproducibility and data transparency.Submit a reproducible project in which code, data, figures, and outputs can be rerun by the marker. Figures should be polished, clearly labelled, transparent about the data, and suitable for inclusion in a research report.
MLO4Demonstrate a comprehensive, practical ability to produce high-quality interactive data visualisations and / or data dashboards that enable users to explore data and gain new insights.Build a working Shiny dashboard with purposeful user controls, at least two interactive plots, and a clear structure that helps users explore behavioural patterns rather than simply displaying static results.
MLO5Demonstrate independence, creativity, and an awareness of practical constraints when selecting and implementing approaches to data visualisation.Choose and justify an appropriate dataset and visualisation strategy. Reflect on trade-offs, limitations, ethical or accessibility issues, data constraints, and how design decisions shaped what users can learn from the work.

How to Evidence the Learning Outcomes

The examples below are not additional assessment requirements. They clarify the kinds of evidence that can demonstrate each criterion and linked learning outcome in a strong portfolio.

Assessment criterionLinked MLOsHow students can evidence thisEvidence markers can look for
Technical ExecutionMLO2, MLO3Organise the submission as a reproducible R project. Use RMarkdown for the report, include the data and any helper files, write code that runs from a clean session, and use appropriate packages for static and interactive visualisation.Report and app can be reproduced; code is readable and efficient; file paths are portable; data import and processing are transparent; outputs are generated from submitted code rather than manual edits.
Visualisation DesignMLO1, MLO3Create four distinct static visualisation types that suit the behavioural questions and data structure. Use appropriate encodings, scales, labels, ordering, colour, faceting, annotation, and theme choices to support accurate interpretation.Visualisations reveal meaningful patterns; design choices are justified; figures avoid misleading scales or clutter; plots are publication-quality; accessibility and readability have been considered.
Use of Behavioural DataMLO2, MLO5Select a behavioural dataset with enough complexity to support multiple visualisation types and an interactive dashboard. Explain what the data represent, why they are suitable, and what behavioural questions the portfolio explores.Dataset is relevant to behaviour; variables and structure are described clearly; data are sufficiently rich for the task; preprocessing decisions are justified; limitations of the dataset are acknowledged.
Interactivity & Dashboard DesignMLO4Build a Shiny dashboard with no more than three tabs, meaningful inputs such as filters or variable selectors, and at least two interactive plots. The dashboard should support exploration and insight generation, not just reproduce the static figures.App runs successfully; inputs update outputs correctly; layout is clear; interactive plots answer useful questions; dashboard supports comparison, filtering, or exploration; user experience is coherent.
Reflection & CommentaryMLO5Discuss the reasoning behind the chosen dataset, visualisation types, dashboard structure, and implementation decisions. Reflect critically on constraints, compromises, uncertainty, accessibility, transparency, and possible improvements.Commentary is specific rather than generic; reflection connects decisions to visualisation principles; practical constraints are recognised; limitations are handled honestly; independence and creativity are visible.
Communication & StructureMLO1, MLO5Write a coherent portfolio that guides the reader through the dataset, visualisations, dashboard, and reflection. Keep the report within the word limit, use clear headings, integrate figures and commentary, and ensure conclusions follow from the visual evidence.Report is easy to navigate; writing is precise and professional; figures and commentary are integrated; conclusions are supported by evidence; presentation is polished and appropriate for postgraduate work.
